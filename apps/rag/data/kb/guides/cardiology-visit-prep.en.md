A little preparation helps you get the most out of a cardiology appointment, whether it is a first consultation, a follow-up after a stent or a check-up for high blood pressure.

## Before the appointment

- **Write down your symptoms.** Note when they started, how often they happen, what you were doing at the time and what makes them better or worse. Palpitations, breathlessness, swelling of the ankles and chest discomfort are all worth describing.
- **List your medicines.** Include doses, supplements and anything you have stopped recently. Bring the boxes if that is easier.
- **Gather previous results.** ECGs, echocardiography reports, stress tests, blood tests (such as cholesterol) and any discharge letters are very useful.
- **Know your family history.** Heart disease at a young age in close relatives is important information.
- **Measure your blood pressure** at home for a few days if you have a monitor, and bring the readings.

## On the day

Wear comfortable clothing that makes an ECG or ultrasound easy. Ask the clinic whether you should avoid caffeine or fasting before specific tests. Bring a family member if you would like help remembering what was said.

## Questions to ask

- What is the most likely cause of my symptoms?
- Which tests do I need, and why?
- What are the options for treatment, and what happens if I wait?
- When should I come back, and which warning signs mean I should seek emergency care?

If you develop severe chest pain, fainting or sudden shortness of breath before your visit, do not wait for the appointment: call emergency services.
