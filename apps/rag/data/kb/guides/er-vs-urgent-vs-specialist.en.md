Knowing where to go saves time and makes sure serious problems are seen quickly. As a general rule, the more sudden and severe the symptoms, the more urgent the care you need.

## Emergency department (ER)

Go to the emergency department, or call an ambulance (123 in Egypt, 112 in Türkiye), for possibly life-threatening problems, for example:

- chest pain with sweating, breathlessness or pain spreading to the arm or jaw
- sudden weakness of the face, arm or leg, or difficulty speaking
- severe difficulty breathing, fainting or a seizure
- heavy bleeding, a serious injury or a suspected broken bone with deformity

Not every hospital has an emergency department, so check before you travel to one.

## Urgent care or a same-day GP appointment

Choose urgent care or a same-day appointment with a general practitioner for problems that need attention today but are not life-threatening: a high fever, a painful ear or throat, a minor cut or sprain, vomiting without signs of dehydration, or a urinary infection.

## Specialist appointment

Book a specialist when the problem is long-standing or already has a known cause, for example recurring headaches, joint pain that has lasted weeks, a skin condition, or follow-up of a heart condition. A general practitioner can help you decide which specialty is right. Many specialists also offer second opinions or telemedicine follow-up.

## If you are unsure

If you cannot decide and the symptoms are severe or getting worse, go to the emergency department. It is always better to be checked and reassured.
