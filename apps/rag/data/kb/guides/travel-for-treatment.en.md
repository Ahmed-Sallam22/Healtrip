Travelling to another city or country for treatment can give you access to a specific specialist or a shorter waiting time, but it needs careful planning. These steps apply whether you are travelling from the Gulf to Cairo or from Egypt to Istanbul.

## Before you book

- **Get a remote review first.** Share your reports and imaging so the specialist can confirm that the trip is worthwhile and estimate how long you will need to stay.
- **Check visa and entry rules.** Some countries offer medical visas or require an invitation letter from the hospital. Allow several weeks for processing.
- **Ask for a written plan.** Request the expected procedure, length of stay, number of follow-up visits and an itemised cost estimate in writing.
- **Check fitness to fly.** Ask your doctor whether flying is safe before and after the procedure, and how long you should wait before the return flight.

## What to bring

Bring copies of your medical records, imaging on a USB drive or via a secure link, a full medication list, and enough medicine for the whole trip plus a few extra days. Keep them in your hand luggage.

## Language and support

Ask whether the hospital provides an interpreter or an international patient desk. Many hospitals offer airport transfers and help with accommodation for a companion.

## Follow-up at home

Before you leave, make sure you receive a discharge summary, the list of medicines you should continue, and clear instructions on warning signs. Arrange a follow-up appointment with a doctor in your home country and share the report with them. Telemedicine check-ins with the treating specialist can help during recovery.
