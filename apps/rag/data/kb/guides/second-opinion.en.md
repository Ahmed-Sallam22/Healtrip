A second opinion means asking another specialist to review your diagnosis or a recommended treatment before you decide. It is common before major surgery, when a diagnosis is uncertain, or when you are considering travelling for treatment. Asking for a second opinion is normal and does not mean you distrust your current doctor.

## How the second opinion process works

1. **Choose a specialist.** Pick a doctor who offers second-opinion reviews in the relevant specialty.
2. **Share your records.** Upload your recent reports, test results and imaging (for example scans, angiograms or MRI reports), plus a short summary of your question.
3. **Remote or in-person review.** The specialist reviews your files remotely, and may suggest a video consultation or an in-person visit if an examination is needed.
4. **Written summary.** You receive a written opinion that explains whether the specialist agrees with the plan, what alternatives exist and which questions to discuss with your treating doctor.

## Timeline

In this prototype, a remote second-opinion review is usually completed within 3–5 working days after all records are received. Complex cases, or missing imaging, can take longer.

## Tips

- Send the original imaging files when possible, not only the written report.
- Write down the specific question you want answered, such as "Is surgery needed now, or can physiotherapy be tried first?"
- Keep your treating doctor informed; the best decisions are made together.

A second opinion is not suitable for urgent symptoms. If you have warning signs such as severe chest pain, seek emergency care first.
