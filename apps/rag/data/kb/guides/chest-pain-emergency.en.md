Chest pain has many causes, from strained muscles and heartburn to problems with the heart or lungs. Some of these causes are harmless, but others need emergency care within minutes. You cannot reliably tell the difference at home, so it is safer to treat certain warning signs as an emergency.

## Red flags: call for help now

Call an ambulance straight away (123 in Egypt, 112 in Türkiye and most of Europe) if chest pain or pressure comes with any of the following:

- pain that spreads to the arm, jaw, neck, back or stomach
- sweating, feeling cold and clammy, or looking pale
- shortness of breath or difficulty breathing
- nausea or vomiting together with chest discomfort
- dizziness, fainting or a feeling that you might pass out
- pain that is crushing, heavy or tight and lasts more than a few minutes

## While you wait

Stop any activity and sit or lie in a comfortable position. Do not drive yourself to the emergency department. If someone is with you, ask them to stay with you, unlock the door and have your medication list ready. Follow the instructions of the emergency operator.

## When it is not an emergency

If chest pain is mild, clearly linked to a movement or a recent injury, and has none of the red flags above, you can usually arrange a prompt appointment with a general practitioner or a cardiologist. If you are unsure, it is always acceptable to seek emergency care. HealTrip cannot assess symptoms remotely and does not replace emergency services.
