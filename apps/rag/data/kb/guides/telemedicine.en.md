A telemedicine consultation is a video or phone appointment with a doctor. It saves travel time and works well for many follow-up visits, but it cannot replace an in-person examination in every situation.

## Good uses of telemedicine

- follow-up after a treatment, a procedure or a hospital stay
- reviewing test results and adjusting long-term medicines
- discussing a second opinion on reports and imaging you have already shared
- first advice on mild, non-urgent problems and deciding whether you need to be seen in person
- mental-health sessions and lifestyle counselling

## When telemedicine is not suitable

Do not use a video consultation for emergencies such as severe chest pain, sudden weakness, difficulty breathing or heavy bleeding. Call emergency services instead. Problems that need a physical examination, imaging or blood tests will usually need an in-person visit.

## Preparing for a video consultation

1. Test your camera, microphone and internet connection, and charge your device.
2. Find a quiet, private and well-lit place.
3. Have your medication list, recent results and any home readings (such as blood pressure or blood sugar) ready.
4. Upload reports or photos in advance if the clinic asks for them.
5. Write down your main questions so nothing is forgotten.

## After the call

Make sure you understand the plan: any new prescriptions, tests to arrange, when to follow up and which warning signs mean you should seek urgent care. Ask for a written summary if one is available. Your privacy matters, so use only the secure link provided by the clinic.
